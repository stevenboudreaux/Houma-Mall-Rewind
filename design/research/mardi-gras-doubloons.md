## 1. What a doubloon is

| Item | Finding | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Form | Coin-shaped carnival throw, thrown from floats; typically aluminium | [W1], [W2] |
| Obverse | Krewe emblem, name and founding date | [E2] (via search summary), [NO] |
| Reverse | Theme of that year's parade/ball, and the year | [E2], [NO] |
| Multiple designs | Some krewes issue several designs a year (officers, attendants, public) | [E2] |
| Made by | Struck from aluminium blanks with metal dies (Sharpe made his own dies) | [W2], [E1] |
| Main maker | Barco Mint, founded 1964 in New Orleans for doubloons, renamed New Orleans Mint in 1996; some krewes now have them struck in China | [W2] |
| Other makers | Osborne Coinage struck many (also Kenner's Star Wars coins) | [SW] |

Check (**guess**): an aluminium disc 39 mm × 1.5 mm at 2.70 g/cm³ weighs ~4.8 g, so 4.5–4.65 g fits a 1.5 mm
rim thickness with the field struck slightly thinner. 15 ga aluminium is ~1.45 mm; 10 ga ~2.6 mm.
Sharpe chose aluminium so the coin could be larger than a silver dollar yet much lighter and safe to throw [64B].

**Edge.** Most parade pieces appear plain-edged (**guess**, from listings titled "plain aluminum"
[EB3], [EB4]). Reeded edges do occur; a Collectors Universe thread discusses a reeded-edge aluminium
doubloon and attributes the effect to soft, thick aluminium being forced into the collar [CUR].

**How anodized colours look (guess, no source found).** Anodizing is a dyed, transparent oxide, so the
metal shows through: a glossy, saturated metallic tint, brightest on polished relief tops, deeper on the
matte struck field. Under raking light the raised lines flash near-white with the tint; recesses read darker
and more saturated. Gold anodized aluminium looks more lemon-brassy than real gold.

**Typical parade colour mix (guess).** Mostly natural silver, then gold, purple, green; occasional red,
blue, pink, copper/bronze-tone (from [E2] and [CC]).

## 4. History

- 1960: Rex throws the first aluminium doubloons, designed by artist H. Alvin Sharpe [W1], [W2], [MNO].
  He demonstrated by throwing one at Rex captain Darwin Fenner, who was unhurt and underwrote the first
  issue [E1], [MNO]. Order: 80,000 aluminium plus 29 silver for members [MNO]; E-Sylum gives 83,000 total,
  80,000 natural and 3,000 gold anodized [E1]. Left undated so they could be reused [MNO].
- Sources disagree on Sharpe's birth (1907 or 1910); one forum post gives 1964 for the first doubloons
  (minority) [CT].
- 1980s: so popular krewes felt they were overproduced, later cut production to about half [NW]. Plastic
  cups from the early 1980s marked a decline of interest in doubloons [64B summary]. Most krewes still throw
  some; it's up to the krewe [DS].

## 6. Business / advertising doubloons

- Yes, they exist: collectors file "advertising" doubloons as a category, from large and small companies;
  Coke, Pepsi and soap tokens named [CU1]. Budweiser Clydesdales 1969 aluminium doubloon [EB8]; Enron
  Energy doubloon via Thibodaux Krewe of Shaka [EB9]; City of Gretna Mounted Police 1975 [EB3].
- **No source found for a restaurant or a mall store issuing its own doubloon** (searched Schwegmann,
  Popeyes, McDonald's, Dixie). A Cuco's doubloon is therefore a **plausible invention, not a documented
  artefact**: the game should present it as a fictional promo / giveaway, or ask the `mall-historian`.
- A modern promo supplier sells blank-to-custom "silver dollar size" aluminium coins, minimum 2,500,
  ~30 business days [UC].

## 7. Collecting

- Crescent City Doubloon Traders Club: ~8 swap meets a year (New Orleans, Gretna, Metairie, Terrytown),
  publishes price guides [E2], [CCD].
- Most aluminium pieces sell for a dollar or less; old silver/bronze and popular krewes are worth more;
  value depends on mintage [E2], [CU2]. Kept in 2×2 holders [CU1]. Collectors follow artists like
  Sharpe and Mike Frolich [CUR]. Archive: Charles V. Booth Collection, UNO [UNO]; Nicholls State archives
  hold krewe doubloon runs, e.g. Krewe of Carrollton 1968–1989 [NA].

## 8. Modelling notes (ALL PROPOSAL / GUESS)

Cuco's is a real mall tenant and its sign is allowed in the game. **Copy no krewe emblem, krewe name or
other third-party logo.** Use only the Cuco's lettering from `design/storefronts/cucos.md` "The neon sign".

| Parameter | Proposal |
|---|---|
| Diameter | 39.0 mm |
| Rim (outer) thickness | 1.5 mm; rim width 1.2 mm, rim top flat |
| Field thickness | 1.1 mm (field recessed 0.2 mm below rim each side) |
| Relief height | 0.15–0.25 mm above field, never above the rim top (coins stack) |
| Edge | Plain, 0.1 mm bevel; optional reeded variant (~120 reeds, 0.15 mm deep) |
| Text ring | Inner ring 3.5 mm wide inside the rim, letters 2.2 mm cap height, raised 0.15 mm, separated from the centre by a thin raised bead line (0.4 mm) |
| Weight check | ~4.6 g in aluminium |

**Obverse (logo side).**
- Centre: the "Cucos" script. Neon becomes **raised lines**: one 0.35 mm raised line per tube run (two or
  three abreast where the sign has two or three tubes), following `cu_sign.json`'s runs, scaled so the script
  is ~26 mm wide. Alternative: the can outlines as raised 0.3 mm walls with the inside struck slightly lower
  and matte (reads as "channel letters").
- Below: the MEXICAN CAFE bar as a raised pill shape with bullnose ends (~28 × 4.5 mm), letters incuse
  (sunk) into it so they read on a coloured finish; the two end stripes as two short raised lines.
- Text ring: e.g. "CUCO'S MEXICAN CAFE" top, "HOUMA, LA" bottom (placeholder). The wording is Steven's to
  write; the mall's own name is left off because the game avoids "Southland" (CLAUDE.md).
**Reverse (theme side).** Krewe-style "theme of the year", e.g. "MARDI GRAS 1995" with a papel picado or
pepper motif, plus a bead border. No real krewe theme copied.

**Finishes (Blender materials).**
- Natural aluminium: metallic 1.0, base ~#C9CCCF, roughness 0.35 field / 0.15 relief tops.
- Anodized sets: periwinkle blue (matches the sign bar), purple, green, gold (~#D8B84A), red; same
  metal, tinted base colour, roughness 0.25, slightly darker in recesses (AO or a curvature mask).
- Collector variant: brass/"bronze" (~#B08D57) or ox silver (dark recesses, polished highs).
- Optional enamel/cloisonné collector piece: brick-red enamel inside the script cans and periwinkle
  enamel in the bar, raised metal lines between colour fields; glossy (roughness 0.05) non-metal enamel.
- No emission: the "neon" exists only as relief; a coloured anodized version can tint the script lines.
